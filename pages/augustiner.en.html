Aurelius Augustinus

Aurelius Augustinus was born on 13 November 354 in Thagaste (today Souk Ahras in Algeria). After an eventful life, marked by intellectual searching and a profound conversion, he became the most important Doctor of the Church in the West.

His Confessions are regarded as the first work of European literature to explore a person’s inner life in such depth. His thought has shaped theology, philosophy and the whole of Western intellectual life to this day.

Augustine died on 28 August 430 as Bishop of Hippo Regius. His legacy lives on in the order that bears his name and in his words, still often quoted today: “Our heart is restless until it rests in you.”

Biography
From teacher of rhetoric to Bishop of Hippo.

Augustine was born on 13 November 354 in Thagaste, the son of Patricius and the Christian Monica. He studied rhetoric in Carthage, where his son Adeodatus was born in 372. Cicero’s “Hortensius” awakened his love of philosophy; at the same time he joined the Manichaeans for about ten years.

After teaching in Thagaste, Carthage and Rome, he became imperial professor of rhetoric in Milan in 384, where the sermons of Bishop Ambrose made a deep impression on him. In August 386 he was converted in a garden when he heard the words “Tolle lege, take up and read”; at the Easter Vigil of 387 Ambrose baptised him together with his son. Shortly afterwards Monica died in Ostia.

In 388 Augustine founded a community of lay monks in Thagaste. In Hippo Regius he was ordained priest in 391 and bishop in 395; even as bishop he lived in community with his clergy, following the example of the first Christians, who were “of one heart and one soul”. Among other works he wrote the “Confessions”, “On the Trinity” and “The City of God” and argued against the Manichaeans, Donatists and Pelagians. He died on 28 August 430 in Hippo, then besieged by the Vandals; his remains have rested in Pavia since the 8th century.

The oldest account of his life was written between 431 and 437 by his friend Possidius, Bishop of Calama, who was at his side when he died.

Timeline

354
Born on 13 November in Thagaste in present-day Algeria. His father Patricius is a pagan, his mother Monica a devout Christian.
361–370
School in Thagaste and Madauros, then a year at home.
370
Studies rhetoric in Carthage.
372
Birth of his son Adeodatus. Cicero’s “Hortensius” awakens his love of philosophy.
373
Turns to the Manichaeans; teacher of the liberal arts in Thagaste.
374–383
Lecturer in rhetoric in Carthage.
383
Moves to Rome; breaks with the Manichaeans and adopts a sceptical attitude for a time.
384
Imperial professor of rhetoric in Milan.
385
Monica comes to Milan.
386
The sermons of Bishop Ambrose and the writings of the Neoplatonists impress him. Around 15 August, conversion in the garden (“Tolle lege – take up and read”). He gives up his professorship and withdraws with friends to Cassiciacum.
387
Baptised by Ambrose at the Easter Vigil (24 April) in Milan. Monica dies in Ostia.
388
Returns to Thagaste and founds a community. His son Adeodatus dies. Writings against the Manichaeans.
391
Ordained priest in Hippo Regius; founds a monastery.
394
Writings against the Donatists.
395
Consecrated bishop as assistant to Bishop Valerius.
396
Bishop of Hippo after the death of Valerius. Writes “On Christian Doctrine”.
397
“Confessions”; the monastic Rule is written around this time.
399
“On the Trinity”.
410
The Visigoths under Alaric conquer and sack Rome.
411
Conference of Carthage; writings against the Pelagians.
413–427
Works on “The City of God”.
416
Under his chairmanship the Synod of Milevis condemns the teaching of the Pelagians.
426–427
“Retractations”, a review of his own work. A successor as bishop is elected.
430
The Vandals besiege Hippo Regius. Augustine dies in the besieged city on 28 August. His relics have been venerated in Pavia since the 8th century.
